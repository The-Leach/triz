Altshuller contradiction matrix

Rows = the factor you want to improve. Columns = the factor that gets worse as a result. Each filled cell holds the principles that most often resolved that trade‑off across the patent record. Click any cell to read them.

Blank cells mean the classical matrix records no strong pattern — that is a signal to reframe the contradiction or treat it as a physical contradiction instead.
The 40 inventive principles

The forty moves Altshuller found recurring across the patent record, each with its classical wording and what it looks like when the thing you are changing is a process rather than a product. You do not need to work through these in order — Solve a problem brings you the handful that fit your contradiction.

No contradiction to work with? Reading all forty and asking “what would this look like here?” is a legitimate TRIZ technique in its own right.
The 39 factors, translated

Altshuller wrote these for engineers in the 1960s, which is exactly why TRIZ feels alien in a service setting. Each factor keeps its original name — so the contradiction matrix still works — with a plain reading of what it means when your “product” is a claim, a patient, a ticket or a customer, and a note on which direction counts as an improvement.
